Best Free and Paid Web Hosting Plans: How to Choose the Right Host

By Joey Technology · 14 November 2026 · 6 min read

Choosing the best web hosting plan depends on what your website needs to run—not simply which provider advertises the lowest price. A static portfolio, a WordPress business website and an online store have different hosting requirements. This comparison explains useful free hosting options, paid providers worth considering and how to choose the right plan.

First, match the hosting type to your website

Hosting is the service that stores and delivers your website files. A static HTML/CSS/JavaScript website has different requirements from WordPress, an online store or a web application with a database. Choose based on the technology your site needs, the level of support you want and how important the site is to your business.

Good free hosting options

1. Cloudflare Pages — best for static websites

Cloudflare Pages is a strong option for static sites, landing pages, portfolios and documentation. It provides a global network and supports custom domains. It does not run a traditional PHP/MySQL WordPress installation on its static hosting product; use Functions or external services when you need dynamic behaviour.

2. Netlify Free — convenient for coded sites and previews

Netlify's Free plan supports Git-based or manual deployments, deploy previews, custom domains and SSL. Its current plan uses a monthly credit allowance, so monitor deploys, bandwidth and requests. This is a good fit for static business sites like portfolios, provided you understand the usage cap.

3. GitHub Pages — useful for project sites and documentation

GitHub Pages publishes static sites from a GitHub repository and supports custom domains. It is excellent for developer portfolios, documentation and open-source project pages. Review GitHub's acceptable-use rules before using it for a commercial business website or transaction-based service.

4. InfinityFree — for learning PHP and MySQL

InfinityFree offers a free PHP/MySQL hosting option that can be used to practise with WordPress and database-backed sites. Free shared hosting has tighter resource, support and reliability limits than paid hosting, so avoid relying on it for a critical business website without understanding its terms and backup arrangements.

Free hosting reality: You may pay nothing for hosting, but you will usually still pay for a custom domain. Free plans can have usage limits, restricted server features, limited support or policies that make them unsuitable for a revenue-critical website.

Reliable paid hosting options to compare

1. Hostinger — accessible all-round option

Hostinger Web Hosting Nigeria offers shared hosting and WordPress-oriented plans with SSL, email and backups depending on the plan. It is worth comparing if you want hosting, domains and basic business email managed from one dashboard. Pay close attention to the upfront subscription term and renewal price.

2. Namecheap — domains and shared hosting in one account

Namecheap Shared Hosting is worth considering for smaller WordPress and PHP sites, particularly if you already manage domains there. Compare storage, backup frequency, email limits and renewal pricing for the exact plan.

3. SiteGround — managed WordPress support and tooling

SiteGround Web Hosting is positioned around managed hosting, WordPress tools, caching and support. It may suit businesses that value managed features, but compare the renewal rate and storage limits carefully.

4. Bluehost — beginner-friendly WordPress hosting

Bluehost Hosting offers hosting packages aimed at websites and WordPress users. Review what is included in the plan, the backup terms, renewal pricing and any add-ons at checkout.

5. DreamHost — WordPress and general-purpose hosting

DreamHost Hosting offers shared and WordPress hosting options. Compare the plan's storage, email arrangements, backup coverage and support model against your website requirements.

6. HostGator — shared hosting for small sites

HostGator Web Hosting is another established shared-hosting provider. Check current plan availability in your country, renewal costs, resource limits and backup terms before purchasing.

Free vs paid hosting: which should you choose?

Choose free hosting when…Choose paid hosting when…
You are learning, testing or publishing a simple static portfolio.The website supports sales, leads, bookings or daily business operations.
You can work within usage limits and self-service support.You need support, predictable resources and clearer service commitments.
Your site does not require a traditional database or server-side runtime.You need WordPress, PHP, MySQL, email hosting or server-side applications.
Downtime or a platform limit would be inconvenient but not costly.Downtime could mean lost enquiries, orders or customer trust.

How to choose a hosting plan

  1. Check the application requirements. Static files, WordPress, PHP, Node.js and databases need different hosting environments.
  2. Compare renewal prices. Introductory discounts are not the long-term cost.
  3. Review backups. Check frequency, retention and whether restores cost extra.
  4. Check support and data-centre options. Choose a provider with support hours and server locations that suit your audience.
  5. Look at limits. Storage, inodes, CPU, memory, traffic and email quotas can matter more than a headline “unlimited” claim.
  6. Keep ownership and backups. Register the domain in your own account and keep an independent copy of your website and database.

Our recommendation

For a coded HTML/CSS/JavaScript site, start with a reputable static host such as Cloudflare Pages or Netlify if the free plan's current limits fit your use. For WordPress or a small business site that needs PHP, databases and managed support, compare paid shared hosting from Hostinger, Namecheap, SiteGround and the other providers above. For an important revenue-generating website, choose reliability, backups and support over the lowest introductory price.
