How to Register a Domain Name: A Step-by-Step Guide for Beginners

By Joey Technology · 14 November 2026 · 6 min read

A domain name is your website’s address—the name customers type into a browser to find your business. Registering one is straightforward, but choosing the right name, comparing renewal costs and keeping ownership under your control are important decisions. This guide explains how to register a domain name in Nigeria or internationally, with links to established registrars.

1. Choose a domain name that is easy to remember

Keep it short, pronounceable and relevant to your business. A brandable name is usually better than a long string of keywords. Check spelling, avoid confusing hyphens where possible, and make sure the name does not infringe another organisation's trademark.

2. Choose the right domain extension

.com is widely recognised for commercial businesses. .ng and .com.ng can communicate a Nigerian presence. Other extensions such as .org, .net, .tech and .store may fit particular organisations or products. Compare the first-year price with the renewal price before deciding.

3. Check availability with a registrar

Use a reputable registrar's search tool. These are useful places to compare availability and registration terms:

  • Namecheap domain search — broad extension selection and domain management tools.
  • Porkbun domain pricing and search — clear extension pricing and privacy features on supported domains.
  • Hostinger domain search — convenient if you plan to buy hosting from the same provider.
  • Cloudflare Registrar — registration and renewals priced at cost for supported extensions; domains use Cloudflare DNS.
  • GoDaddy domain search — a large registrar and domain marketplace.
  • NiRA — the registry authority for Nigeria's .ng namespace; use its accredited registrar directory to find a registrar for .ng domains.

Availability and supported extensions vary. Search the exact name you want and compare the full cost, not just the promotional headline.

4. Compare the real cost before checkout

Check the registration price, renewal price, transfer fee, taxes, currency conversion and any optional add-ons. A very low first-year offer can renew at a much higher rate. WHOIS privacy may be included, paid or unavailable depending on the extension and local registry rules.

5. Register the domain in your own account

  1. Create an account with the registrar.
  2. Search for your chosen domain and select the correct extension.
  3. Choose the registration period and review the renewal price.
  4. Enter accurate registrant and contact information.
  5. Enable two-factor authentication and domain lock where available.
  6. Pay and verify the email address if the registrar requests it.
Ownership tip: Register the domain using an email address and account controlled by you or your business. If a developer helps, grant delegated access rather than allowing the domain to be registered in their personal account.

6. Connect the domain to your website

After registration, open the DNS management area and add the records supplied by your hosting provider. This often means an A or ALIAS record for the root domain and a CNAME for www, but follow the exact instructions from your host. Nameserver changes and DNS updates can take time to propagate. Enable HTTPS/SSL after the domain is connected.

7. Protect and renew your domain

Turn on auto-renew only when your payment method is reliable, keep account recovery details current, enable registrar security features, and record the expiry date in your calendar. A domain is rented for a registration term; it is not a one-time purchase.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a name before checking trademark conflicts.
  • Buying only because the first-year offer looks cheap.
  • Registering the domain under a developer's personal account.
  • Ignoring renewal reminders or letting the payment card expire.
  • Changing DNS records without saving the original settings.
