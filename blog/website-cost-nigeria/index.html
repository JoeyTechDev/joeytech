How Much Does a Business Website Cost in Nigeria? What Drives the Price

By Joey Technology · 9 November 2026 · 3 min read

If you are planning a business website in Nigeria, the first question is usually "How much will it cost?" The honest answer is that it depends on what the website has to do. A five-page company profile and a booking platform with payments are very different projects, even if both are called "a website".

What drives the cost of a business website

  • Scope. The number of pages, and how much content each page needs.
  • Design. A customised template costs less than a fully custom design built around your brand and customers.
  • Features. Online booking, payments, customer portals, and member areas each add build and testing time.
  • Content. Copywriting, photography and product data take time, whether you or the developer produce them.
  • Integrations and automation. Connecting forms to a CRM, WhatsApp, email or a payment gateway is extra work, but it is often where the business value is.
  • SEO and performance. Proper page structure, speed optimisation and tracking should be part of the build, not an afterthought.

Costs people forget

The build is not the only cost. Plan for domain renewals, hosting, business email, paid plugins or licences, and ongoing website maintenance. A site that is never updated gradually becomes slower and less secure.

Template, WordPress or custom?

Most business sites do well on WordPress, which is easy to edit and affordable to extend. Sites with logins, dashboards or complex rules are usually better as custom applications. We explain the trade-offs in WordPress vs custom web development.

Questions to ask before accepting any quote

  1. Exactly which pages and features are included?
  2. Who owns the domain, hosting account and website files?
  3. Is the site designed for mobile first?
  4. Does the price include SEO setup, analytics and forms?
  5. How many rounds of revisions are included?
  6. What support do I get after launch?

Warning signs

  • A price with no written scope.
  • No mention of mobile performance or speed.
  • The developer keeps ownership of your domain or hosting account.
  • No handover, training or documentation.
